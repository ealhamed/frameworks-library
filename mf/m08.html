The core idea

Digital transformation is not the IT project it is budgeted as; it is a change in where the firm ends. Platforms let a company orchestrate customers, agents and collaborators it does not employ, with data as the coordinating asset. The AIA case shows an insurer building an ecosystem beyond policies — and the management problem shifting from running functions to aligning supply and demand across a boundary that moves. — after the AIA ALive case

The hero diagram

The firm as orchestrator.

In an ecosystem the centre does not own the nodes; it aligns them. The asset that does the aligning is data — who sees what, and who acts on it.

Frameworks in this module

Named ideas to remember.

Transformation as Systemic Change · CD61 live session 6
Not a tool swap: incentives, roles and boundaries move · Legacy channel and platform coexist and conflict · Sequencing and coalition matter as much as tech
A digital initiative that changes no one's decision rights or incentives is an IT upgrade wearing transformation's badge.
Data as Strategic Asset · AIA ALive case
Proprietary behavioural data compounds · Value realised only when it changes decisions · Ecosystem partners trade access for data
Data earns the word "asset" only when a named decision improves because of it — otherwise it is cost with a roadmap.
Dynamic Supply-Demand Alignment · CD61 live session 6
Demand sensed in real time across the ecosystem · Supply flexes through partners, not just capacity · The platform captures value from the match
Ecosystems beat pipelines when matching, not making, is the scarce skill — the boundary of the firm moves to wherever the match is made.
Platform-Ecosystem Polymorphism · CD61 Module 8 handout
Integration binds parts through ownership · Platforms bind sides through network effects · Ecosystems bind partners through supermodular complementarities · Co-opetition separates joint value creation from competitive value capture
A firm can operate across all three forms; choose the governance mechanism that matches how value is actually created, rather than calling every digital boundary a platform.

How to apply

Assessing a digital initiative as a boundary decision.

  1. Ask whose behaviour must change. List the roles — internal and external — whose daily decisions the initiative assumes will change, and what is in it for each.
  2. Trace the data loop. What is sensed, who sees it, which decision it feeds, and how the outcome flows back. A broken loop means no compounding.
  3. Check the channel conflict. AIA's agents were partners AND incumbents. Name who loses under your platform and how they will be carried or compensated.
  4. Decide where the firm now ends. Own, partner, or orchestrate — make the boundary choice explicitly instead of inheriting it from the org chart.
