The core idea

Bhutan caps tourism not by fiat headcount but by price: a daily levy keeps volume low and value high, trading revenue today for heritage, environment and brand tomorrow. It is a measurement argument as much as a moral one — what you count (GDP, arrivals, quarterly profit) quietly decides what you optimise. Growth pursued through every stakeholder tension eventually meets one it cannot pay back. — after the Bhutan pre-read

The hero diagram

The Bhutan choice.

Volume and value per visitor are levers every business holds, not just kingdoms. Most competitors crowd into the high-volume cells; the deliberate cell is rarer and harder to copy.

What is measured gets optimised · Delayed costs hide in unmeasured stocks · Coyle: progress needs better counting
Before accepting a "profitable" strategy, ask which stock it is drawing down — trust, environment, staff, brand — and when the bill arrives.

How to apply

Auditing what your growth is quietly spending.

  1. List what you measure weekly. That list, not the mission statement, is your real strategy.
  2. Name the unmeasured stocks. Reputation, staff energy, customer trust, ecosystem health. Ask which are being drawn down to hit the measured numbers.
  3. Find your Bhutan lever. Is there a price, standard or cap that would trade volume for value your competitors cannot match?
  4. Sort your stakeholder tensions. Which are genuine trade-offs, and which could design dissolve? Only escalate the first kind.
