m.09 · II · Risk, valuation & financing · Optimal Financing

Capital Structure: MM to Trade-off

Hennessy · MM is a benchmark, not advice: leverage matters only through taxes, distress and incentives.

Idea Diagram Frameworks Apply Reading

The core idea

Modigliani–Miller proves that in a perfect market the debt–equity split cannot change firm value — slicing the pizza differently bakes no more pizza. That is the benchmark, not the advice. Taxes tilt toward debt by the PV of the interest tax shield; financial distress costs push back; debt overhang shows over-levered firms rationally skipping good projects. The trade-off theory sets leverage where the marginal shield meets marginal distress. — after Hennessy, Lecture on Financing

Put simply · Slicing pizza and playing with fire.

MM’s insight: cutting a pizza into more slices does not create more pizza. Splitting the same business between lenders and shareholders cannot by itself change what the business is worth.

The real world adds two things. The tax office subsidises borrowing — interest is deductible, so debt bakes a slightly bigger pizza. But too much debt and the kitchen catches fire: customers flee, talent leaves, good projects die. Optimal leverage is where the subsidy still beats the fire risk.

Frameworks in this module

Named ideas to remember.

Modigliani–Miller Irrelevance · Modigliani & Miller, 1958
Perfect markets · V_L = V_U · Leverage repackages risk, not value
If leverage seems to create value, name the imperfection doing the work — MM guarantees there is one.
Interest Tax Shield · MM with taxes
Interest deductibility · PV of shield ≈ τ·D (perpetual) · Captured in WACC
Debt’s advantage is exactly the PV of taxes not paid — no more, and only while there is profit to shield.
Trade-off Theory & Debt Overhang · incl. Myers, 1977
Distress costs cap leverage · Optimal D/E at the margin · Overhang: NPV>0 projects skipped
Past the peak, leverage destroys value twice — through expected distress and through the good projects the overhang kills.

How to apply

Sizing leverage for a real firm.

  1. Start from MM and name the frictions. Taxes, distress, agency. If none apply, the financing debate is theatre.
  2. Estimate the shield honestly. It is only worth τ·D while profits exist to shield — cyclical firms carry smaller effective shields.
  3. Price distress before it prices you. Customers, suppliers and engineers flee a shaky balance sheet long before default.
  4. Check for overhang. If new projects mostly bail out old lenders, shareholders will rationally starve good investments.

Key reading · Berk & DeMarzo · Corporate Finance, Ch. 14–16

Capital structure from irrelevance to trade-off, with the proofs.

The clean derivation of MM, the tax-shield arithmetic, and the distress-cost evidence the lecture compresses into a curve. Read Chapter 16 for why the peak of that curve is wide, not a point.
