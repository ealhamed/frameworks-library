The Pecking Order

Hennessy · Profitable firms borrowing least is the fact the trade-off theory cannot explain — the pecking order can.

Idea Diagram Frameworks Apply

The core idea

Managers know more than markets, and financing choices leak that knowledge. Issue equity and investors infer the shares are dear — the price drops on the announcement itself. So firms fund in order: internal cash, then debt, equity last. It explains why the most profitable firms borrow least. Agency costs cut both ways: debt disciplines managers who waste free cash, and tempts shareholders near distress into asset substitution. — after Hennessy, financing & distress lectures

Put simply · Why selling shares feels like a used-car ad.

You know your car better than any buyer. So the moment you volunteer to sell, buyers wonder what you know that they do not — and bid less. Companies issuing shares face the same suspicion: management knows the business best, so “we’re selling equity” reads as “we think it’s expensive”.

That is why firms spend their own cash first, borrow quietly second, and only sell shares when they must. The order of the queue is itself the message.

Frameworks in this module

Named ideas to remember.

Pecking Order · Myers & Majluf, 1984
Asymmetric information · Adverse selection in equity · Internal → debt → equity
Selling shares tells the market you think they are overpriced — so equity is the financing of last resort.
Issuance as Signal · signalling literature
Announcement effects · Equity issues → price drop · Buybacks & debt → confidence
The financing announcement is information before a single dollar moves — price the signal, not just the security.
Agency Costs of Debt & Equity · incl. Jensen, 1986
Free-cash-flow discipline · Asset substitution near distress · Value of financial slack
Debt is both the discipline that stops empire-building and the hazard that tempts gambling — dose accordingly.

How to apply

Choosing how to fund the next big move.

  1. Exhaust boring money first. Retained earnings carry no announcement effect and no fees. Use them.
  2. Price the signal before the security. An equity issue at a "fair" price still costs the announcement drop. Budget for it.
  3. Keep slack for the option value. Unused debt capacity is the ability to move fast later — worth real money in a downturn.
  4. Watch incentives near distress. Levered shareholders love long shots with lenders’ money. Covenants exist because of you.
