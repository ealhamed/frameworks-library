Bond Pricing & the Yield Curve

Hennessy · Price and yield move inversely — that single fact is interest-rate risk.

Idea Diagram Frameworks Apply

The core idea

Debt is the cleanest laboratory for discounting: promised coupons, a face value, a date. Price each cash flow at the zero-coupon spot rate for its maturity — one flat yield is an approximation that breaks whenever the curve slopes. Yield to maturity summarises the price; it does not set it. Above the risk-free curve sits the credit spread: the market’s live estimate of default. — after Hennessy, Lecture on Debt Markets

Put simply · Why old bonds go on sale.

A bond is an IOU with fixed payments. If new IOUs start paying 5% and yours pays 3%, nobody buys yours at full price — it has to go on sale until its buyer earns 5% too. Rates up, price down. Always.

The yield curve is just the price of waiting, listed by how long you wait. And the extra yield on a company IOU over a government one is the market whispering how likely it thinks you are to not pay.

Frameworks in this module

Named ideas to remember.

Price–Yield Inversion · bond arithmetic
Fixed promised flows · Rate up → price down · Duration scales the move
Rates and prices are two ends of one seesaw — longer duration just means you are sitting further from the pivot.
Term Structure / Spot Rates · after Hennessy
Zero-coupon rates by maturity · Discount each flow at its own rate · Curve shape carries expectations
Use spot rates, not one flat yield, to discount a stream — the curve is the market’s price list for time.
Credit Spread · ratings & markets
Corporate yield − govt yield · Default probability × loss · Ratings as coarse bins
The spread is the market’s live default estimate — it reprices daily while ratings move in quarters.

How to apply

Reading a debt quote like a corporate treasurer.

  1. Split the yield in two. Risk-free for the maturity, plus spread. Only the spread is about your firm.
  2. Check duration before judging risk. A 2% rate move hurts a 10-year bond roughly five times more than a 2-year.
  3. Never discount a stream at one flat rate. When the curve is steep, flat-rate pricing misprices every leg — arbitrage lives in that gap.
  4. Watch spreads, not ratings. The market downgrades weeks before the agencies do.
