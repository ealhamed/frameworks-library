m.02 · I · Investment decisions · Computing Cash Flow

Free Cash Flow

Hennessy · Cash flow is not net income — the gap is interest, depreciation, working capital and capex.

Idea Diagram Frameworks Apply

The core idea

The number you discount is free cash flow: after-tax operating profit, plus non-cash charges back, minus what you must reinvest in capex and working capital. Two disciplines produce it. Count only incremental flows — the ones that exist because the project does. And weight by probability: a launch case that ignores the failure branch is a forecast of luck, not of cash. — after Hennessy, Cash Flow lecture

Put simply · Profit is opinion; cash is fact.

Accountants spread big purchases over years and book sales before the money arrives. Useful for reports, useless for deciding whether a project pays. You can only reinvest actual cash, so you rebuild the number: real money in, real money out, in the year it actually moves.

And you only count money that moves because of this project. The money you already burned deciding? Gone either way. It gets no vote.

The hero diagram

From operating profit to the number you discount.

Five moves, in order. Every valuation in this course starts by walking this bridge.

  1. 1
    EBIT
    Operating profit before financing. Interest stays out — financing lives in the discount rate, not the numerator.
  2. 2
    × (1 − τ)
    Tax the operating profit as if unlevered. The debt tax benefit is captured later, in WACC.
  3. 3
    + D&A
    Depreciation is an accounting memory of old spending, not a cash outflow. Add it back.
  4. 4
    − CAPX
    Real cash out the door for plant and equipment, counted when spent — not when depreciated.
  5. 5
    − ΔNWC
    Inventory and receivables tie up cash as sales grow; recover it when the project winds down.

Frameworks in this module

Named ideas to remember.

Free Cash Flow Identity · standard corporate finance
EBIT(1−τ) · + D&A · − CAPX · − ΔNWC
Discount unlevered cash flow — put financing effects in the rate, never in the numerator, or you count them twice.
Incremental Cash-Flow Principle · after Hennessy
Ignore sunk costs · Charge opportunity costs · Charge cannibalization
Value the world with the project minus the world without it — nothing else belongs in the model.
Expected Cash Flow · probability weighting
Scenario flows · Probabilities · Probability-weighted mean
Discount the expectation across scenarios, not the single case the sponsor happens to believe in.

How to apply

Auditing a sponsor’s spreadsheet.

  1. Find interest in the cash flows. If it is there, the model double-counts financing. Send it back.
  2. Hunt the sunk costs. R&D already spent and consultants already paid do not belong. Delete them without mercy.
  3. Charge the project for what it steals. The land it uses, the sales it takes from your existing product — both are real costs.
  4. Ask for the downside branch. One scenario is a story. Probability-weight at least two before you call it an expectation.
