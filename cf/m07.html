Enterprise DCF

Hennessy · Equity value is Enterprise Value minus net debt — the discipline is not double-counting.

Idea Diagram Frameworks Apply

The core idea

Company valuation is project valuation grown up. Forecast unlevered free cash flow, discount at WACC, close the horizon with a terminal value — that is enterprise value, the worth of operations to all claimants. Subtract net debt for equity. Multiples are the cross-check, not the method. And the standard machinery silently assumes diversified owners and liquid shares; control, illiquidity and tunneling adjustments exist because reality often breaks both. — after Hennessy, Company Valuation Summary

Put simply · Price the whole house, then subtract the mortgage.

To value a company, first price the whole business as if nobody owed anything — add up the cash it will generate, shrunk back to today. That is enterprise value: the house.

The shareholders do not own the house free and clear; the bank has a claim. Subtract the mortgage (net debt) and what is left is the equity. Most valuation disasters are someone accidentally counting the mortgage twice — or forgetting it entirely.

The hero diagram

From forecast to equity value.

The order is not negotiable. Most valuation errors are steps done out of sequence or counted twice.

  1. 1
    Forecast FCF
    Unlevered free cash flow over an explicit horizon — usually five to ten years of defensible line items.
  2. 2
    Terminal value
    A growing perpetuity (or exit multiple) for everything beyond the horizon. Routinely most of the value — treat g with suspicion.
  3. 3
    Discount at WACC
    The blended rate from Module 6. The debt tax benefit lives here, not in the cash flows.
  4. 4
    Enterprise Value
    The operations, priced for all claimants. Cross-check against comparable-company multiples.
  5. 5
    − Net debt → Equity
    Debt minus cash, at market value. What remains belongs to shareholders.

Frameworks in this module

Named ideas to remember.

EV–Equity Bridge · standard valuation
Enterprise value · Net debt · Equity value
Value operations for everyone, then subtract the lenders’ claim — mixing the two levels is the classic double-count.
DCF vs Multiples · after Hennessy
Intrinsic: FCF + WACC + terminal value · Relative: EV/EBITDA, P/E of comparables · Triangulate
DCF gives the reasoning, multiples give the market check — a valuation quoting only one is half a valuation.
Ownership Adjustments · control & liquidity effects
Control premium · Minority & illiquidity discounts · Tunneling risk
Standard DCF assumes diversified owners and liquid shares — price the deviation when valuing stakes, not textbook firms.

How to apply

Reviewing a valuation before you sign it.

  1. Find the terminal value share. If 80% of value sits past the forecast, the model is mostly one growth assumption. Stress it.
  2. Check g against gravity. A firm growing faster than the economy forever eventually is the economy. Cap g accordingly.
  3. Hunt the double-count. Interest in the FCF, or debt subtracted twice — the two commonest ways to destroy a model quietly.
  4. Triangulate with multiples. If your DCF says 14× EBITDA and peers trade at 8×, the burden of proof is yours.
