m.03 · I · Investment decisions · Alternative Investment Criteria

IRR and Its Traps

Hennessy · IRR and NPV agree only for a conventional, standalone project — this module is about the exceptions.

Idea Diagram Frameworks Apply

The core idea

IRR is the discount rate that sets NPV to zero, and boards love it because it looks like a return. It breaks twice. Non-conventional cash flows produce multiple IRRs, or a borrowing-type project where a high IRR is bad news. And for mutually exclusive projects, the higher-IRR project can be the smaller-NPV one. Rank by NPV, or by incremental IRR — never by standalone IRR. — after Hennessy, Lecture on IRR

Put simply · A percentage that flatters.

IRR answers "what return does this project earn on itself?" Sounds perfect — until you notice that a tiny project can earn a huge return on almost nothing, while a big project earns a decent return on a lot. The second one buys the yacht.

NPV counts money, not percentages. When the two disagree, money wins.

The hero diagram

When can you trust an IRR?

Two questions decide it: does the sign of the cash flows flip more than once, and are you ranking rivals? Only one cell is safe.

Frameworks in this module

Named ideas to remember.

IRR Rule & Failure Modes · after Hennessy
NPV(r*) = 0 · Multiple sign changes → multiple roots · Lending vs borrowing type
An IRR is only a return if the project is lending-type with one sign change — check the pattern before quoting the number.
Incremental IRR · standard treatment
Difference the cash flows · IRR of the increment · Compare to cost of capital
To choose between rivals with IRR at all, compute the IRR of the difference — or just use NPV and go home early.
Payback & Profitability Index · practitioner screens
Years to recover outlay · PV per unit of capital · Capital rationing
Payback is a liquidity screen and PI a rationing tool — neither is an investment rule on its own.

How to apply

When someone leads with “the IRR is 30%”.

  1. Count the sign changes. More than one — decommissioning costs, staged outlays — and the 30% may have siblings.
  2. Ask lending or borrowing. If cash comes in first and goes out later, you want a LOW IRR. Most audiences have never checked.
  3. Refuse IRR rankings. Scale and timing distort them. Mutually exclusive projects get ranked by NPV, full stop.
  4. Keep payback in its lane. Fine as a cash-at-risk screen. The moment it vetoes a positive-NPV project, it is destroying value.
