The core idea

Nike shows the mechanics bite: book versus market weights, stale versus current yields, which beta — each choice moves the valuation, and the analyst’s error is using any of them uncritically. Marriott shows one firm-wide WACC misallocating capital across three different-risk divisions. Yahoo shows a firm whose value is mostly stakes in other firms: value the parts, subtract, and read what the market thinks the core is worth. — after the Nike, Marriott & Yahoo! case set

Put simply · One thermostat for a house with three climates.

Marriott ran hotels, contract services and restaurants — three businesses with different risk. Using one company-wide discount rate is like one thermostat for a greenhouse, a freezer room and a lounge: every room ends up wrong.

Nike adds the quieter lesson: two analysts with the same data can build different WACCs, and the construction choices — not the formula — decide the verdict. Yahoo adds the party trick: subtract the pieces a company owns of other companies, and see what the market pays for the actual business. Sometimes it is almost nothing.

How to apply

Running the case method on your own firm.

  1. Rebuild your house WACC from scratch. Market weights, today’s yields, a beta you can defend. Compare to the official number.
  2. Ask who wins under one hurdle rate. The riskiest division always loves the firm-wide WACC. That enthusiasm is the tell.
  3. Sum the parts of anything diversified. Stakes, excess cash, non-core assets — value separately, then see what the core is really priced at.
