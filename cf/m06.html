m.06 · II · Risk, valuation & financing · WACC & Company Valuation

WACC

Silva · WACC is the cost of the firm’s average asset. The trap is using it for projects that are not average.

Idea Diagram Frameworks Apply

The core idea

Debt and equity holders each require a return; WACC blends the two at market-value weights, with debt tax-adjusted because interest shields profit. It discounts unlevered free cash flow into enterprise value. The trap is scope: WACC prices the firm’s average risk. For a division, a private firm, or an unusual project, unlever a pure-play comparable’s beta, relever to the target, and build the rate from there. — after Silva, WACC and company valuation

Put simply · The blended interest rate on everything you owe everyone.

A company runs on two kinds of money: borrowed (cheaper, and the tax office gives a discount on the interest) and shareholders’ (pricier, because they get paid last). WACC is the blended rate across both, weighted by how much of each you use.

Any project must clear that blended bar or it makes your investors poorer. One catch: it is the bar for your average project. Using the company-wide bar for a wildly different venture is how safe divisions end up subsidising reckless ones.

The hero diagram

The blended price of capital.

Market-value weights, always. The (1 − τ) is the tax shield showing up in the rate — never in the cash flows too.

WACC = (E/V)·r_E + (D/V)·r_D·(1 − τ)

Frameworks in this module

Named ideas to remember.

WACC Identity · standard corporate finance
Market-value weights · Cost of equity via CAPM · After-tax cost of debt
Blend at market values and tax-adjust the debt leg — book weights and coupon rates both produce confident nonsense.
Unlever / Relever Beta · pure-play method
Comparable equity beta · Strip its leverage → asset beta · Relever at target D/E
Asset betas travel across firms; equity betas do not — always move risk through the unlevered form.
Divisional Cost of Capital · after Silva
Per-division comparables · Division-specific WACC · Firm WACC ≠ cost of assets
One firm-wide rate subsidises risky divisions and starves safe ones — capital flows to the wrong place by construction.

How to apply

Building a WACC that survives review.

  1. Weight at market values. Equity at market cap, debt at market or fair value. Book equity is history, not price.
  2. Build r_E from a relevered asset beta. Comparables → unlever → median → relever at the target capital structure.
  3. Take r_D from current yields. The rate the firm would pay today — not the coupon on debt issued years ago.
  4. Match the rate to the asset. New business line? Different country risk? Rebuild the WACC; do not reuse the house number.
