The core idea

Modern money is inconvertible and intrinsically useless — a promise that works because taxes must be paid in it and everyone expects it to keep working. Central banks issue only the monetary base; commercial banks multiply it through lending, which is why banking crises are monetary events. Inflation should be low and stable — currently 2% — and while money growth drives inflation in the long run, the short run has many parents. — after the CD56 Module 5 material

Put simply · The ten-pound note that becomes a hundred.

You deposit £100. The bank keeps £10 in the vault and lends £90 to a baker, who pays a builder, who deposits it again. The bank keeps £9, lends £81. Run that forever and your £100 has become £1,000 of money in accounts — created not by the government, but by banks chasing loan profit.

That is most of what money is: promises multiplied by confidence. Which is why a banking panic is not just a finance story — it makes money itself vanish.

And inflation? A little, steady — 2% — keeps the machine oiled. Too much burns savings; falling prices jam the gears entirely. Central banking is mostly keeping the porridge at exactly the boring temperature.

The hero diagram

The quantity theory, handled with care.

Money times velocity equals prices times output — an identity, not a lever. Velocity moves, the central bank does not control the relevant M, and short-run inflation answers to costs and expectations too. In the long run, Friedman wins.

Frameworks in this module

Named ideas to remember.

Functions of Money · monetary economics canon · Wallace on fiat
Medium of exchange · Store of value · Unit of account · Legal tender + tax demand as the anchor
Fiat money is inconvertible and intrinsically useless; it works because someone always needs it to settle a debt the state will enforce.
The Money Multiplier · CD56 Module 5 · Brunnermeier & Koby on the floor
Outside money: cash + reserves · Inside money: deposits from lending · Multiplier = 1/r — until banks stop lending · Reversal rate: where cuts shrink lending (Repullo: bank-specific; euro-area sub-zero still worked)
£100 of base becomes £1,000 of deposits at a 10% reserve ratio, or £0 when lending freezes — most money is a private-sector decision.
Goldilocks Inflation · the 2% consensus
Stable and low: costs vanish, planning works · High: shoe-leather and distorted prices · Deflation: also costly, policy paralysis · The 2% itself: born in a 1988 New Zealand TV remark, hardened into convention
Target low AND stable — far from hyperinflation, safely clear of deflation, with room for policy to manoeuvre.
The Flat Phillips Curve · Del Negro, Lenza, Primiceri & Tambalotti, 2020
Inflation decoupled from the cycle since the 1990s · Cause: muted pass-through of cost pressures, anchored expectations · MMT's opening: Kelton's deficits-until-inflation, Cochrane's rebuttal
Flatness is a policy achievement, not a law of nature: credible central banks earned it, deficits leaned on it, and 2021 showed it can snap back.
The Future of Money · Reichlin · Rey · Portes, 2025 · Fernández-Villaverde on competition
Bitcoin: fails the means-of-exchange test · CBDC: risk-free digital cash, cautious central banks · Stablecoins: fast, programmable, run-prone · Currency competition disciplines the sovereign, yet under-supplies money
The live contest is CBDC versus stablecoins; run risk and reserve opacity are the referee. Bitcoin is an asset, not money.

How to apply

Reading a monetary headline without being fooled.

  1. Ask which money grew. Base money and broad money can move in opposite directions — QE proved a bigger base is not automatically more money in the economy.
  2. Check the multiplier's health. If banks are not lending, monetary expansion dies in reserves. Watch credit, not just the central bank's balance sheet.
  3. Split long run from short run. Sustained money growth means inflation eventually. This quarter's print answers to energy, supply chains and expectations.
  4. Take deflation seriously. Falling prices read as a discount and act as a disease — debt burdens grow, spending waits, policy loses traction.
  5. Judge new money by the old tests. Medium of exchange, store of value, unit of account. Apply the three functions to any token before the whitepaper applies its adjectives.

Key reading · Reichlin · Project Syndicate, September 2025

Europe Needs a Euro Stablecoin.

Reichlin — who teaches this course's central-banking session — argues stablecoins are inevitable: they provide programmable settlement rails a cautious CBDC will not, so the policy question is who issues them and against what reserves. Read alongside Rey on dollar dominance and Portes on multi-issuer run risk, it turns the course's money-multiplier logic loose on the newest form of inside money.

Private money is not new — deposits were always private promises. Stablecoins just changed the wrapper.
