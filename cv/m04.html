Channel Design

Session 3 · how channels create value, the direct-vs-indirect trade-off, and what e-commerce and subscriptions actually change.

Idea Diagram Frameworks Apply

The core idea

A channel is not logistics; it is the part of the product the customer experiences last. Intermediaries earn their margin by providing reach, assortment, and local trust you cannot cheaply replicate. Going direct buys back control, data and margin — and hands you inventory, fulfilment and acquisition costs. E-commerce and subscription models redraw the trade-off: the first shortens the path, the second turns a sale into a relationship. — after the CD57 Module 4 material

Put simply · Selling honey: farm gate or supermarket shelf?

Sell your honey at the farm gate and you keep every pound, meet every customer, and reach almost nobody. Put it in supermarkets and you reach everyone — but the store takes its cut, owns the shopper data, and can drop you next season.

Neither is right; they buy different things. The web let you ship jars from your kitchen, and subscriptions turned honey into "a jar every month" — same product, but now the game is keeping the customer, not finding them.

The hero diagram

Control x reach: the channel trade-off.

Direct and indirect are not rivals but positions on two axes. Most mature firms end up hybrid — deliberately or by accident.

How to apply

Choosing (or fixing) a route to market.

  1. List the functions your customer needs at purchase. Advice, availability, returns, financing. Then ask who performs each best.
  2. Price the direct dream honestly. D2C margin looks fat until you subtract fulfilment, returns and paid acquisition.
  3. Map the conflict before adding a channel. Your retailer notices your webshop. Decide the rules — pricing, exclusives — before they decide for you.
  4. For subscriptions, watch churn before growth. Acquisition with leaky retention is renting customers at a loss.
